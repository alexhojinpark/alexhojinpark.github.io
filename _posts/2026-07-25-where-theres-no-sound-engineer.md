After my day job, I volunteer as a sound engineer for a ministry that travels between churches to help run their services. Over time I’ve worked on all kinds of mixing consoles in all kinds of rooms, diagnosing sound problems, fixing them on the spot, and advising the teams there. It’s been meaningful work. But I kept running into the same situation everywhere I went.

> There was always a band. But there was almost never a sound engineer.

At a live event, the sound engineer handles the very last stage before the sound reaches the audience. No matter how good the musicians are, without someone to shape that final mix, it rarely reaches people as good sound. I’ve felt this from the other side too, as a musician: when there’s an engineer you trust, you stop worrying about your levels and just play. That trust is as much psychological as technical, and it frees the whole band to play with more confidence. When no one is running sound, that weight falls back on the musicians, who are already busy enough playing.

But skilled sound engineers are scarce. The craft takes years to learn, and finding someone who can show up every single week is harder still. I sat with this problem for a long time. Could I solve it with what I’d experienced, what I’d studied, and what I actually cared about?

Then a simple thought occurred to me:

> Plenty of people can’t operate a mixing console, but almost everyone can operate their computer.
>
> So what if you connect the two, and let the computer take over the engineer’s job?

And that’s the new journey I’m starting. My goal goes one step further than simple automation. I want to build an automixer for live sound, one that can drive a real console in real time, tuned for the environments where it’s needed most.

This project started in a worship setting. But standing on a stage with no dedicated engineer isn’t a problem unique to small churches. It’s the same for small venues, new bands, and community events everywhere.

In my next post, I’ll introduce more about my background, what I’ve studied, and what I’ve experienced and learned so far, along with the technical approach behind how I’m actually going to build this and solve the problem. This blog is where I’ll document that journey.
